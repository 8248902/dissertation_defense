# Defense presentation instructions

These rules cover the slides (`core.tex`, `backup.tex`), the talk notes (`\talknote`), `defense-script.md`, the Anki cards, and the delivery guide.

## Positive framing

State what a result shows, its direction, magnitude, or practical effect. Do not frame a statement by what it is not ("names a result, not a topic", "not a benchmark result", "does not claim ..."). If a negative observation adds no information, remove it. Keep negation only when it is the precise content: a hypothesis, a counterexample, an implementation rule, a direct answer to an anticipated question, or an evidence limit that changes how the result is read. After editing, reread the passage for negative framing, including wording without "not" ("only", "need not", "fails", "remains to be").

## Present only the current work

Never suggest that an experiment, measurement, or benchmark needs to be re-run, repeated, or remeasured, in any wording ("rerun", "remeasure", "needs fresh measurements", "repeat before citing"). Never refer to historical, legacy, prior, earlier, old, previous, or superseded versions of this work's own formulations, conventions, implementations, or experiments, and avoid "current" when it implies an older version. Present the method and its results as the method and its results. If something was not measured, leave it out rather than flag it. Citations of other authors' prior work and mathematical uses (a Bayesian prior, an earlier slide or iterate) are fine.
