Corrections to the review
------------------------
1. M1: the sharper temporal bound is correct for any positive diagonal Lambda.
   Write ell=lambda_min^2, K=A^T A+Lambda^2. Then
     K^-1 A^T A K^-1 <= K^-1-ell K^-2 <= (4 ell)^-1 I.
   Thus ||K^-1 A^T|| <= 1/(2 lambda_min). The ratio of the old stated
   bound to this bound is 2||A||/lambda_min, not universally 4/lambda_min.
   Using ||A||<=2 gives the advertised 2,000,000 versus 500 when the floor
   is 0.001. These are upper bounds, not measured amplification factors.
   The older bound is conservative, not literally vacuous. In the review's
   verified-items list, 1/(4 lambda^2) bounds SQUARED noise gain; amplitude
   gain is bounded by 1/(2 lambda). A finite chain need not attain either.
   The displayed kernel table actually spans CN 20.22–1375.75, rather than
   22–1376: the review overlooked the Gaussian row at 20.22. The separate
   Ch2 worst-case condition-number calculation at a ceiling of 100 is valid.

2. M2: the displayed expression is an admissible coercivity lower bound,
   not necessarily the optimal coercivity constant. For fixed positive
   alpha, beta_B and C_P, its small-m leading coefficient is
     min{1/(2|Omega|), alpha^2/(2|Omega| beta_B C_P^2)}.
   Therefore c0~m/(2|Omega|) is not generally valid. Its dependence on the
   common coefficient class and independence from b are correct and useful.

3. Q-S1: the Gaussian interpretation and discrepancy-only likelihood-ratio
   identity are valid for the fixed, unweighted model. Include the prior
   normalization if writing the density explicitly:
     -log p_gamma(y) = C_gamma(y) + .5 log det M_gamma
                       - .5 log det M0_gamma + constant.
   The extra terms are candidate-independent only with fixed common model
   parameters and dimensions. The conditional mean is P^-1 T y_L with
   precision 2P. For general diagonal Lambda it is a linear predictor,
   not necessarily a scalar multiple or Euclidean contraction of y_L;
   scalar Lambda gives attenuation mode by mode. The operator contracts in
   the P metric. Comparing densities of different candidate observations is
   not by itself a generative model or MAP theorem for disparity.
   C_gamma-C_0=D is an exact algebraic identity; using D as a new score is
   unevaluated. An ideal identical profile minimizes D when gamma>0 and A
   has full row rank; identical competitors still tie.

4. Q-F2: replacing quadratic H1 regularization by TV needs a separate
   analysis. Integrated positive definiteness alone does not transfer the
   abstract H1 theorem with arbitrary b to a TV energy. For example choose
   B=I on part of Omega and zero on an open set U, and a nonzero smooth
   vector field w compactly supported in U. With b=t w and t sufficiently
   large, E(s w)=s[alpha TV(w)-t||w||^2] tends to minus infinity as s grows.
   Nevertheless integral B is positive definite. Residual-square fidelity
   has additional structure; suitable TV models do have existence results.
   TV also need not lose uniqueness when fidelity is strictly convex.
   The backup explains the modeling tradeoff without claiming a new BV theorem.

5. C1: the SAR kernel can contain additional spatial modes; it need not
   strictly exceed the constants for every projection family. The exact
   characterization in prop:ch2_sar_solvability is the appropriate claim.
   This proposition uses lambda I, not lambda^2 I, in its normal equations.

6. Q-T5: temporal reconstruction has a separate unknown offset at EACH
   pixel. A single global affine normalization of an image generally does
   not remove that nullspace. Historical PSNR preprocessing must be traced
   before claiming that it does. Q-F8 and Q-S3 likewise require actual
   run evidence; the observed zero field does not identify its cause, and
   typical normalized data norms cannot be guessed.

7. Dissertation-side observations are mostly useful qualifications rather
   than defense errors. An arbitrary L1 driver supplies W^{1,1}, not H1;
   the particular finite-width bounded kernels may have stronger regularity.
   Half-open interval conventions should agree with the chosen primitive.
   Chapter 5 states gamma>0 as a sufficient SPD hypothesis; that statement
   is true but unnecessarily restrictive, since gamma=0 also works. These
   manuscript passages are outside the requested presentation edits.
