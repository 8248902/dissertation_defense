## Required corrections

### A1. Regression bias bound needs common-mean noise

Target: `core.tex:209–214`, frame “Estimating a time-surface gradient: a bias–variance tradeoff.”

The slide assumes independent equal-variance timestamp errors, but its bias bound omits their means. Independence and equal variances alone allow spatially varying error means. In that case the slope bias gains `Q^{-1} Σ_j z_j E[n_j]`, which is not bounded by the displayed Hessian remainder. The source proposition assumes i.i.d. noise and explicitly permits a **common** mean, which symmetry removes.

Suggested replacement: “For a centrally symmetric full-rank design and independent errors with common mean and finite variance σ²…” State that the true timestamp surface is C² with L_H-Lipschitz Hessian on a neighborhood containing the sample disk/segments. The quantitative bound and covariance are otherwise correct. The takeover sentence should preferably say “A larger nested neighborhood reduces variance…” because arbitrary larger-radius nonnested sample choices need not increase the design matrix in PSD order.

Source: `shared_bodies/ch3_eb_hs_of_body.tex:156–204`, `prop:ts_regression_bound`, particularly D1/D2 and part (iv).

### A2. State all discrete graph hypotheses

Target: `core.tex:306–317`, frame “Discrete flow: connectivity and sampled directions matter.”

As written “symmetric weighted graph” does not assert nonnegative weights, and “connected” permits a one-vertex graph with zero degree. The theorem also uses PSD data blocks and α>0 inherited from the preceding continuous section. Make them visible in this theorem:

“Let α>0, q_i>0, B_i=B_iᵀ≽0, and let w_ij=w_ji≥0 define a connected graph with d_i=Σ_j w_ij>0.”

Then `Σ_i q_i B_i ≻0` gives both discrete uniqueness and the specific block-Jacobi convergence claim. The q_i extension is valid: define `C_i=q_i B_i` and reuse the PSD-block theorem with C_i. For the spectral proof use

`D_i=α²d_i I+C_i`, `R_ij=α²w_ij I`,

`zᵀ(D∓R)z=Σ_i z_iᵀC_i z_i+α²Σ_{edges}w_ij|z_i∓z_j|²`.

Zero modes of D−R are constant fields. Zero modes of D+R are alternating constants on a bipartite component, or zero on a component with an odd cycle. `Σ_i C_i≻0` removes any nonzero remaining constant vector. Consequently the symmetric similar Jacobi matrix has spectrum in (−1,1). This correctly proves convergence and does not appeal to the false general implication “SPD implies Jacobi convergence.”

Sources: C3:829–837, 890–920, `cor:vox_projection_wellposed`, `thm:convergence`, `cor:convergence_psd_blocks`.
