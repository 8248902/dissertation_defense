\section{Temporal reconstruction}
\begin{frame}{Events fix each pixel's brightness increments and leave one constant free}
\begin{columns}[c]
\column{.50\textwidth}
{\small\color{muted}Observation at one pixel}\par
Choose a unit-mass temporal kernel $\phi_{s_k}$:
\[e(t)=\sum_{k\text{ at this pixel}}p_k\phi_{s_k}(t),\]
\[y_i=\int_{t_i}^{t_{i+1}}e(t)\,dt.\]
\column{.46\textwidth}
{\small\color{muted}Increments of the unknown history}\par
\[\A=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1&&\\&-1&1&\\&&\ddots&\ddots\end{pmatrix},\]
\[\A\mathbf f\approx\mathbf y,\qquad\ker\A=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1\}.\]
\end{columns}
% source detail: Ch. 2, eq:exy, eq:Exy, eq:tempdiffA, lem:nullspace.
\src{\dref{Eq.}{eq:Exy}, \dref{Eq.}{eq:tempdiffA}, and \dref{Lemma}{lem:nullspace}.}
\talknote{Temporal observation model; 75 seconds.}{Suppress the pixel index to keep the notation readable. The algorithm chooses the reconstruction times $t_i$; the penalty on the next slides chooses the reference level. Integrating a unit-mass kernel across a bin assigns all or part of each event to that bin. Delta binning has no spread; Gaussian, rectangular, and spline kernels distribute influence over time. The entries of A are unscaled increments: A f lists the differences of consecutive samples.}{If Af=0, every consecutive pair of components is equal, hence f is constant. Conversely A1=0. Under ideal threshold calibration the observed increments approximate changes in L/c\_th, with kernel-boundary, overshoot, and between-event endpoint errors. No absolute frame is used to set the baseline. Scene terms: each pixel's brightness history is rebuilt on its own; a pixel without events stays at the reference level.}
\end{frame}

\talknote{Sensor-to-observation model; about 90 seconds. Answers ``why is $\A\mathbf f\approx\mathbf y$ a model of an event camera?'' before it is asked.}{Under the ideal threshold rule each event marks a log-brightness change of one threshold, so the signed count between two times telescopes to the change in $L/c_{\rm th}$: $y_i\approx(L(t_{i+1})-L(t_i))/c_{\rm th}$. The dashed curve is the reference at the last event. The exact identity from Chapter 2 names the three discrepancies. $B$ is the kernel-boundary residual: kernel mass that spills across $t_i$ or $t_{i+1}$; it is zero for delta binning when no event lies on a boundary. $\varepsilon_\ell=\Delta_\ell-c_{\rm th}p_\ell$ is the overshoot at each recorded event. $r(t)$ is the unreported change since the most recent event; in the diagram it is the gap at $t_{i+1}$.}{Assumptions (P1)--(P4): unit-mass kernels, kernels localized relative to event spacing, $|\varepsilon_\ell|\ll c_{\rm th}$, and positive intensity. A missed or spurious event makes some $\varepsilon_\ell$ comparable to $c_{\rm th}$, which is outside (P3). The regularized solution adds a fourth discrepancy, the data residual: $f^*_k-f^*_i=y(t_i,t_k)+\sum_{\ell=i}^{k-1}\rho_{{\rm res},\ell}$, and larger weights enlarge it. Subthreshold signals can be invisible over a whole window: discussion page ``The physical event stream loses more than one baseline''. \begin{frame}{A unique, stable estimate for every positive weight}
\small
For fixed $\Lam=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i)$ with every $\lambda_i>0$,
\[\mathbf f^*=\arg\min_{\mathbf f\in\R^n}\ \tfrac12\norm{\A\mathbf f-\mathbf y}^2+\tfrac12\norm{\Lam\mathbf f}^2,\qquad
\K_{\rm time}=\A^\top\A+\Lam^\top\Lam\succeq\lambda_{\min}^2\mathbf I.\]

\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\bfseries}p{2.6cm}p{5.1cm}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash\leavevmode\color{muted}}p{5.4cm}@{}}
Unique & $\K_{\rm time}\mathbf f^*=\A^\top\mathbf y$ has one solution & \\
% source: Ch. 2 prop:stability, ||A||_2 <= 2; one event changes y by at most 1 in norm.
Stable & $\norm{\delta\mathbf f^*}\le(2/\lambda_{\min}^2)\norm{\delta\mathbf y}$ & one spurious event moves a history by at most $2/\lambda_{\min}^2$\\
% source: Direct consequence of Ch. 2 thm:uniqueness normal equations and A1=0; supplementary identity.

\begin{frame}{Integrated directional information gives a unique flow in $H^1$}
\begin{block}{Unified well-posedness theorem}
Let $\Omega\subset\R^2$ be bounded, connected, and Lipschitz; $\alpha>0$.
Assume
\[\B\in L^\infty,\quad \B=\B^\top\succeq0\text{ a.e.},\quad b\in L^2,\qquad
\setlength{\fboxsep}{1.5pt}\boxed{\int_\Omega\B(\mathbf x)\,d\mathbf x\succ0}.\]
Then $E_{\B,b}$ has a unique minimizer in $H^1(\Omega;\R^2)$.
\end{block}
\vspace{1mm}
\small Nonparallel edges control constant velocities; smoothness supplies motion where $\B=0$.
% source detail: Ch. 3, thm:unified\_wellposedness, eq:integrated\_identifiability.

Connectedness makes the kernel of the gradient consist of global constants. The positive regularization coefficient controls spatial variation. Bounded data curvature and L2 forcing make the weak forms bounded. Positive definiteness of the integrated B controls constant fields that smoothness cannot see.}{B may vanish on open subsets while its integral remains positive definite. For time surfaces, no nonzero vector can be perpendicular to all measured gradients almost everywhere. The condition need not hold pointwise.
