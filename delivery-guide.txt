THE STORY
  Events report changes, so the observations leave directions of the unknown
  uncontrolled. A temporal penalty selects a baseline. Spatial smoothness
  controls flow fluctuations, while integrated directional data control the
  mean. A fixed-motion SAR model bridges temporal reconstruction to flow by
  linking per-frame changes to one scene. Stereo adds another question: a
  unique latent solve does not ensure the correct correspondence. The profiled
  cost exposes that distinction.

  Introduce the threshold diagram before inverse-problem notation. Define
  optical flow as apparent image-plane velocity, a time surface as crossing
  time, a voxel as a spatial-temporal bin, and disparity as a horizontal offset.
  The talk assumes familiarity with linear algebra and variational analysis,
  rather than familiarity with event cameras or computer-vision algorithms.

MATHEMATICAL PRESENTATION CHOICES
  A always denotes the unscaled temporal first difference. The flow data matrix
  is B (A_data in the dissertation). State fixed-weight hypotheses before
  showing normal equations. Separate coefficient stability from physical-motion
  accuracy, and local candidate selection from the complete spatially aggregated
  stereo estimator.
  The flow theorem needs integrated positive definiteness, not full rank at
  every pixel. The proof gives a quantitative coercivity bound. Contrast
  maximization has a finite-dimensional existence result, not a convergence
  guarantee for the local solver. The SAR kernel can contain other invariant
  scene modes; do not claim they always occur.
  The gamma/conditioning equality assumes equal blocks and scalar
  regularization. Exact stereo profiling and selection concern the unweighted,
  candidate-local model. Brouwer gives an IRLS fixed point; Banach adds
  convergence only under a sufficient condition. No candidate-specific
  normalized data norm is available to certify that condition for the runs.
  Ch. 2 leaves a separate additive temporal offset at each pixel. The historical
  PSNR normalization and alignment were not recovered, so do not claim that a
  global image normalization resolves this per-pixel null space. The known-motion
  figures establish that the time-surface output is zero in the shown scenes;
  available staged artifacts do not establish why.
